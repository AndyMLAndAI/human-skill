# Mode: Normal (email, Slack/Teams, cover letters, everyday messages)

## Email
- Target 50–125 words for a normal transactional email. If you're past 150, cut.
- No "I hope this email finds you well," "I hope you're having a productive week," "I wanted to reach out regarding." Open with "Hey [Name]," or go straight into the ask.
- Sign off with "Thanks," "Best," "Cheers," or initials. Never "Warmest regards," never "Please do not hesitate to reach out."
- Prose paragraphs (1–3 sentences), not bullets — reserve bullets for genuinely 4+ discrete items.
- Example transform:
  - AI (188 words): apologizes twice, explains the conflict at length, "delve deeply into the product roadmap," offers options with bold labels, thanks profusely.
  - Human (38 words): "Hey Marcus, Caught in an emergency Q3 budget audit tomorrow afternoon and won't make 2pm. Any chance we push to Friday morning at 10 or afternoon at 2:30? If not, happy to do Monday. Sorry for the shuffle, Alex"

## Slack / Teams
- 1–2 sentences, under 25 words, for a normal ping.
- Integrate the greeting with the ask on the same line — don't send "Hi" and wait (the "no hello" norm).
- Lowercase starts and relaxed punctuation are fine here.
- AI failure mode: turning a quick check-in into an 80-word memo with an emoji wave and "please let me know if you need a second pair of eyes!" Just ask the thing.

## Cover letters
- Cut the "multifaceted professional" archetype, hyperbolic passion ("I have long admired your trailblazing innovation"), and buzzwords (spearhead, leverage, holistic, elevate).
- Replace with specific metrics and real constraints: what broke, what you actually did, the number that resulted. Acknowledging a mess you inherited and fixed reads far more credible than claiming frictionless excellence.
- Example: "Our team inherited a messy self-hosted Kubernetes cluster that failed every other Tuesday. I led the migration to EKS with Terraform: cut AWS spend by $28k/month, reduced deploy rollbacks from 14% to under 2%." — not "I leveraged my multifaceted skill set to drive transformative infrastructure outcomes."

## General principle
Everyday writing is judged on how little of the reader's time it wastes. If a sentence doesn't change what the reader needs to do, cut it. Bloat reads as AI; brevity reads as a person who respects your time.
