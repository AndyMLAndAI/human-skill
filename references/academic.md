# Mode: Academic

For essays, papers, lit reviews, research writing.

## Hedge with exact boundaries, not vibes
Bad (AI): "It is crucial to consider that while these findings are promising, several limitations must be acknowledged. Dietary factors are multifaceted and influenced by numerous variables."

Good (human): specifies the actual statistical boundary — confidence intervals, sample restriction, measurement method, what it does NOT generalize to. E.g. "the protective association attenuated after adjusting for baseline socioeconomic status... exposure was assessed via self-administered questionnaires prone to recall bias... cannot be generalized beyond [specific cohort]."

Rule: every hedge must name a specific confound, instrument limitation, or sample boundary. A hedge that could be pasted into any paper on any topic is a fake hedge — cut it or make it specific.

## Cite dialectically, not as a survey
Don't just summarize what each source says and imply they all agree. Use citations to dispute a methodology, point out an omitted variable, or stake out a distinct position.
- AI flattening: "Mercer and Klein (2018) highlight pivotal trends. Stiglitz (2020) emphasizes stability. Together these studies underscore the complex tapestry of forces."
- Human friction: "Mercer and Klein's fixed-effects panel fails to control for simultaneous currency shocks — relying on aggregate FDI flows conflates speculative liquidity shifts with actual divestment."

## Jargon must be load-bearing
Every technical term should be operational/falsifiable (specific stats, thresholds, methods) — not decorative ("transformative paradigm shift," "intricate interplay of pivotal factors"). Test: if you replaced the term with "important" or "complex" and lost nothing, it was decorative — cut it.

## Don't smooth over failure
Real research has failed replications, noisy instruments, inconclusive assays, contradictory data. Say so plainly instead of spinning it into "exciting opportunities for future optimization." State what didn't work and what that means for the claim you can actually make.

## Structure
Argument doesn't need to be a rigid five-paragraph shape. Let dense analytical paragraphs sit next to short, blunt ones. Avoid the compulsion to "balance" every claim with an immediate counter-claim in the same breath — take the position the evidence supports.
